## Part 3 — carousel

### The render-path decision

The Week 2 kit renders carousels with **Wyren MCP** (`imageAI`). It was not connected in
this project — no `mcp__wyren__*` tools, no `.mcp.json` — and reconnecting meant
`npx wyren-mcp`, a browser approval, and credits per render.

Chose **HTML/CSS screenshotted by headless Chrome** instead. Reasoning: Trueline is
typography-led. It publishes exact peso figures, uses two specific typefaces, and bans
cartoon and mascot styling. An image model mangles small text and will not hold `#C4622D`
across nine slides. HTML renders text as text and a copy fix is a one-line edit.

### Diagram-led, not photo-led

The two available photographs are clean architectural roofs. Neither shows rust, a lifted
panel, or a ceiling stain, and one is roofer-red — banned by the brief. Illustrating "rust
around a tekscrew" with a photograph of something else is the exact dishonesty the brand
argues against.

So each of the six symptom slides got a **hand-drawn SVG cross-section** naming the real
part: tekscrew, EPDM washer, purlin, ridge roll, side lap, canal. Copper appears on nothing
except the failure and the price.

### The brief's slide count was wrong

`brief.md` says "8 slides", then lists 1 hook + 6 signs + 1 counterweight + 1 CTA = 9.
Built all nine rather than dropping a sign or merging the counterweight into the CTA — the
counterweight is the trust asset. Instagram allows 20.

### Things that broke, and the fixes

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| `₱` fell back to a system font mid-string | Google's `latin` subset excludes U+20B1. Added **`latin-ext`** to `build-fonts.py` |
| Dead vertical gap under one-line titles | Made `.plate` `flex:1` so it absorbs slack and the fact rows land at the same height on every slide |
| Diagrams floating small inside oversized plates | `.plate svg{width:100%;height:100%}` so the drawing scales to the box |
| Slide 6 ridge cap crossed the panel lines | Redrew the apex, lifted the cap, filled the sealant void in translucent copper |
| Slide 7 canal too small and off-centre | Redrew larger and centred; label moved below the drip |
| Slide 5 lift too shallow to read | Deepened the wedge from 48px to 74px at the tip |
| `₱410,000` — peso glyph collided with the 4 | `letter-spacing:.06em` on a `.peso` span |
| Slide 1 photo invisible | Scrim was at .82–.97 opacity. Restructured into a photo band over solid slate so the headline never sits on texture |

### Unresolved

The **six per-symptom peso ranges are new numbers.** `brief.md` publishes the band
(₱6,500–₱75,000, average ₱16,800) but no per-symptom prices. Set them inside the band with a
plausible average. Rommel signs off before anything is posted — the positioning rests on
those numbers being real.

## Part 3 — Meta ads

### Three ratios from one source

Meta carousel cards are **strictly 1:1**; feeding 4:5 slides in centre-crops them and cuts
the price line off every card. Rather than maintain a second set of files, `slides.css`
switches layout on **viewport-height media queries**, so the same HTML renders 4:5, 1:1 and
9:16 depending on the Chrome window size.

```
python carousel/render.py            # 4:5   feed
python carousel/render.py --square   # 1:1   carousel cards
python carousel/render.py --story    # 9:16  Stories + Reels
```

### The placement correction — Kenn was right

I initially recommended **Manual placements, Feed only**, excluding Stories and Reels to
avoid a 1:1 card sitting pillarboxed in a 9:16 frame.

Kenn pushed back and asked why Stories and Reels were excluded. He was right and I was
wrong: that optimises for the creative you already have rather than the placement worth
having. It throws away the cheapest inventory on the platform and constrains the auction to
dodge a problem that is really a missing asset.

Corrected to **all placements except Audience Network**, and built the 9:16 set — padding
type into the middle band because Meta covers roughly the top 14% and bottom 20% of a story
with its own UI. Photography bleeds past it; type never does.

Audience Network stays off deliberately: third-party apps off Meta, and a known source of
accidental clicks on a click-optimised campaign.

Also learned: **Reels does not accept carousel ads.** Draft 1 will show Reels greyed out.

### Four drafts, one variable at a time

| Draft | Format | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| `six-signs-carousel` | Carousel, 5 cards | Educational / contrarian |
| `story-7800` | Single image | Testimonial |
| `taglish-380k` | Single image | Taglish interrupt |
| `the-18-peso-part` | Single image | Curiosity, one specific part |

2 and 3 deliberately share a creative so **copy** is the only variable; 4 swaps the image to
test a diagram against a photograph. All four live in **one campaign, one ad set** so they
share an audience and the results are comparable.

## Infrastructure

### The destination URL was a blocker

`brief.md` listed the landing page as a `claude.ai/code/artifact/...` link. A paid click
landing on a sign-in wall is a dead click, so the page needed a real public home.

Deployed `site/index.html` to **Vercel production** — deliberately not a preview URL, since
Vercel previews often carry Deployment Protection and would have reproduced the same
login-wall problem. Verified anonymously before using it: 200, correct title, images
serving, no auth wall.

**https://trueline-roof-care.vercel.app**

### GitHub, and a check worth doing

Created a public repo for the whole capstone and connected it to Vercel, so every push to
`main` redeploys.

`vercel.json` at the repo root sets `outputDirectory` to `site`. Committed config rather
than a dashboard Root Directory setting, so the deploy behaviour is version-controlled with
no invisible state.

Then verified what the Git-triggered build **actually served**: root returns
`site/index.html`, and `brief.md` returns **404**. Worth checking — serving the repo root
would have published the brief, the lead CSV and every build sheet on the ad's destination
domain.

Also: `vercel link` had written a live OIDC token to `site/.env.local`. Confirmed the staged
file list was clean before the first commit.

## Part 4 — emails

Two emails, one design system: shared masthead, copper button, callout, sign-off, slate
footer. Email-safe throughout — tables, inline CSS, 600px single column, no JavaScript.

**Georgia, not Fraunces.** Webfonts do not load in Gmail, Outlook or Yahoo. Shipped
`'Fraunces', Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif` so Apple Mail gets Fraunces and everyone
else gets Georgia — and designed against Georgia, since that is what most people see.

### The masthead failure, and what it taught

The header started as a hosted PNG on the Vercel site. Kenn's test email arrived styled but
**with no banner**.

Diagnosis, in order:

1. Re-checked the URL — 200, `image/png`. Not our file.
2. Found a real bug of mine on the way: `font-size:0` on the masthead cell meant that when
   images *were* blocked, the alt text was invisible too. So any failure showed a blank gap
   rather than a fallback. Fixed.
3. Kenn confirmed **the image was present in the GHL editor** — so GHL had not stripped or
   rewritten it. That isolated it to Gmail's own image policy.

Rather than keep chasing a client-side setting, **rebuilt the masthead as live HTML text**
on a slate cell — wordmark, copper rule, tagline. Nothing to block, proxy, or rewrite. Cost:
the roof photograph is gone from the header. Worth it. Re-tested and it rendered.

The diagram in email 2 stays an image, since it is supporting content, and now degrades to
styled alt text on a chalk panel.

**Rule going forward: email headers for this brand are type and colour, never a remote
image.**

### Getting them into GHL

Kenn drives GHL through a **Claude browser extension**. Handing it a prompt that pointed at
the **public raw GitHub URL** worked first time for both emails — far better than pasting
180 lines into a chat. The prompt has to say explicitly: replace don't merge, and do not
reformat or prettify (inline CSS, MSO conditionals and the hidden preheader all look like
mistakes to a tidying agent).

Email 1 is live in **A2 — Appointment Confirm & Show-Up** and its test arrived styled.

## The gap we found at the end

Building **A1 — Speed-to-Lead**, the designed trigger is `Form Submitted`. Kenn said he had
no form. Checking `site/index.html` confirmed it: **every CTA links straight to the booking
widget.**

Which means a visitor who does not book never becomes a contact. Two consequences:

1. The nurture email's audience — "landed but didn't book" — cannot currently exist.
2. **No segment tags exist**, so none of the `repair` / `care-plan` / `multi-unit` /
   `new-build` branching the whole system is built around can run. Every segment would get
   the homeowner nurture.

Took the fast route to close Part 4 — trigger A1 on `Contact Created` as a stand-in — and
wrote the proper fix up as **`ghl-form.md`**, including a paste-ready extension prompt.

When the form exists, **replace** A1's trigger rather than adding to it: a form lead is also
a newly created contact, so both triggers would fire and send the nurture twice.
