# script.md — the spoken version

**How to use this.** The blocks marked 🎯 are worth learning close to word for word — the open,
each section's value line, and the close. Everything else is bullets on purpose: you'll be
looking at a live screen and reacting to it, and prose written in advance for that always sounds
like prose written in advance.

Rehearse twice out loud. If you stumble, keep going — a confident rough take beats a nervous
polished one, and Loom viewers forgive far more than you think.

~660 words of speech. At a natural pace that's **4:15–4:45**, inside the 3–6 minute bar.

Voice rules from `../brief.md` still apply: no exclamation points, no ALL CAPS, English throughout.

---

## 0:00–0:25 · Slide 1 🎯 Learn this one

> Hi, I'm Peter Gutierrez, and I build lead-generation systems.
>
> Not just a landing page, and not just ads. The whole machine that turns a stranger into a
> booked call — the page, the ads that feed it, the CRM sitting behind it, and the follow-up
> that runs whether or not anyone remembers to send it.
>
> I'm going to show you a complete one I built in a week for a roofing company in Laguna. It's
> a real system, and every part of it is something I can build for you.

**Why it opens this way:** capability first, no login screen, and the viewer knows inside ten
seconds why they're watching.

---

## 0:25–0:45 · Slide 2

Four parts, named once so they know the shape of what's coming. **Don't read the cards.**

- A page that converts
- The ads that feed it
- The CRM behind it
- Follow-up that runs itself
- 🎯 *"Any one of these is worth something. Wired together they compound — that's the part most people don't build."*

---

## 0:45–1:35 · Slide 3, then the live page

Scroll it steadily. Talk about the decisions, not the sections.

- The promise in the hero: the truth about your roof, in writing, within 24 hours — **even when the truth costs us the job**
- **The prices are published.** Almost nobody in this market does that. It's a positioning decision, not a design one
- The form near the bottom asks one question that matters: *what do you actually need?*
- 🎯 *"I can build you a page that earns the click it paid for — and that turns the people who aren't ready to book yet into leads instead of losing them."*

---

## 1:40–2:20 · Slide 4, then the rendered creative

> ⛔ **Do not open Ads Manager.** Show the finished creative from `../carousel/out/` and the copy
> from `../ad-drafts.md`. The drafts inside Meta are part-built, and this is the section meant to
> prove range — don't put the least-finished thing on screen during it. Nobody watching a
> capability pitch wants a tour of the Ads Manager UI; they want to know whether you can make an
> ad that works.

**On screen:** the nine carousel PNGs full size, then the same card at 4:5, 1:1 and 9:16 side by
side, then the four hook angles from `../ad-drafts.md`.

- Nine slides. Founder story hook, six signs, then the counterweight and the call
- **One creative source, three ratios** — feed, square, Stories — so nothing gets a bad crop on any placement
- Four ad drafts, four different hook angles against the same offer, so there's something to actually test
- 🎯 *"I can build the creative and write the copy, not just place the buy. Most people can do one or the other."*

---

## 2:25–3:05 · Slide 5, then the pipeline board

- Six stages, fifteen real leads, real values on the board
- **Lost isn't a stage.** Dead deals keep their position and get a status and a nurture tag, so the board shows you *where* money dies instead of hiding it in a graveyard column at the end
- The testimonials on that landing page use names from this board — 🎯 *"the page, the pipeline and the emails are one consistent world"*
- 🎯 *"I can set you up a pipeline that tells you where deals actually stall, instead of one that just stores names."*

---

## 3:10–4:10 · Slide 6, then A1's canvas → the email

**The most important minute in the video.** Canvas first, then cut to the rendered email and
let it sit for a beat before you say anything.

- Someone fills in the form. Inside sixty seconds they get a reply and a task lands for the owner
- Then it **branches on who the lead actually is** — 🎯 *"a landlord with twelve rental roofs should never get an email about one ceiling stain. Same system, different conversation."*
- If they still haven't booked, it follows up on its own for four days and then closes the file and tags them for the next season
- **[cut to the email, full screen, pause]** 🎯 *"And the follow-up it sends looks like this."*
- 🎯 *"That's the part that runs at nine on a Sunday night when nobody's at their desk."*

**If asked, or in passing:** the SMS layer is built and sitting behind A2P registration, a US
carrier requirement that catches every new sub-account. It works the day that clears.

## 4:10–4:45 · Slide 7 🎯 Learn this one

> So that's the system: a page, the ads that feed it, a CRM behind it, and the follow-up wired
> across all of it.
>
> If you're running a business and any of that sounds like something you're missing — I can
> build it for you. Your CRM set up so every lead lands in one place. The page and the funnel.
> The ads. And the follow-up that runs on its own.
>
> The link's on the screen. Book a call with me and tell me what you're trying to fix, and I'll
> tell you honestly whether I'm the right person to fix it.
>
> Thanks for watching.

**Why it closes this way:** a specific next step, and the last line quietly mirrors the promise
the demo business makes. That's not an accident — say it like you mean it, because it's the
thing that separates you from someone who just finished a course.
