## Who you are (identity)

**Business:**
Trueline Roof Care — a **Santa Rosa, Laguna** roofing company working across Santa Rosa, Biñan,
Cabuyao, Calamba, San Pedro, Los Baños, and Sta. Cruz. Three things, and only three:

1. **Diagnose and repair** existing pre-painted long-span GI, corrugated, and tile roofs
2. **Maintain** them on annual care plans — single homes and multi-unit buildings alike
3. **Install** roofing on new construction, quoted off the plans with a free takeoff

What we don't do is talk anyone into a tear-off they don't need. Founded 2019 by **Rommel
Bautista** — 19 years on roofs, nine of them running re-roofing crews for a contractor in
Cavite and Laguna.

**What you stand for:**
Most roofs condemned in Laguna have years left in them. They get torn off because re-roofing is
where the money is — ₱180,000 to ₱450,000 a house — not because the roof is finished. What was
actually wrong was forty loose tekscrews, three dead rubber washers, and a ridge roll nobody had
sealed since 2016. And the same instinct shows up on new builds, where owners get one number
scrawled on a notebook page with no breakdown of gauge, framing, or square meters.

Trueline exists to make roofing legible. Every roof gets a written diagnosis or a written
takeoff, itemized, with real numbers — and if the honest answer is *"hindi pa kailangan,"* we
say it out loud and make nothing.

**What makes you different:**
1. **We repair 7 out of 10 roofs we're called to replace.** Published number. No competitor
   in Laguna publishes theirs.
2. **Free written quotation in 24 hours — itemized.** Labor, materials, gauge, and price per
   square meter, broken out line by line. Yours to keep, yours to compare against any other
   contractor. On new builds that means a **free roofing takeoff from your plans** — sheet
   count, purlin runs, fasteners, accessories, wastage — the same document a project estimator
   would produce, at no cost and no obligation.
3. **We refer out re-roofing for zero commission.** Two vetted contractors. When a roof is
   genuinely finished we hand you off and make nothing — the proof the other 70% is honest.
4. **Our money is in ₱6,500–₱75,000 repairs, ₱8,500/yr care plans, and new installs**, not
   ₱400,000 panic tear-offs, so the incentives point exactly where we say they do.
5. **Laguna-based, year-round, same crew.** No Manila subcontractors, no crews that vanish
   after the rainy season, no *"nandiyan kami sa subdivision ngayon"* door knocking.

## What you want (the goal)

**The goal:**
Booked 15-minute Roof Check calls that convert into on-site inspections or plan reviews, then
into paid repairs, annual care plans, and new-build installs. Every layer — ad, page, calendar,
pipeline, automation — is measured against one number: **booked calls that turn into paid work.**

**Who it is for:**

**A — The second-opinion homeowner (primary, ~65% of leads — all ad copy leads here)**
35–60, owns a 10–25 year old house in a Laguna subdivision. Household income
₱90,000–₱280,000/month. There's a brown stain spreading on the Hardiflex ceiling, a panel that
lifted during the last habagat, or a contractor who quoted ₱380,000 for a full re-roof. Not
panicked — **suspicious.** Can afford the re-roof and resents being pushed into it before anyone
has explained what's actually wrong. Also includes the **maintenance planner**: no active leak,
wants it checked before the rains, happy to pay a few thousand a year to stop thinking about it.

**B — The multi-unit owner or property manager (~20%)**
Owns or manages apartment rows, townhouse blocks, dormitories near UPLB, or staff housing near
the Laguna industrial parks. 6–60 units, several roofs, and a leak in unit 4 every rainy season.
Thinks in maintenance budgets and tenant complaints, not in emergencies. Wants one contractor
who inspects every roof on a schedule, reports in writing, and doesn't disappear. **This is the
recurring-revenue segment** — one signature covers a dozen roofs.

**C — The new-build owner or small contractor (~15%)**
Building a house, a duplex, an apartment block, or a small commercial building somewhere in
Laguna. Has plans, has a budget, and has one or two vague quotes that don't break anything down.
Needs to know what the roofing actually costs — sheets, purlins, fasteners, accessories, labor,
per square meter — before committing. Often the owner, sometimes the GC or architect. Comes to
us for the **free takeoff** and stays for the install.

**Not for:** industrial cladding at scale, concrete deck waterproofing, genuine end-of-life
roofs (referred out), and anyone shopping purely for the cheapest labor-only crew.

**The offer:**
A free roof assessment and a **free itemized written quotation within 24 hours** — an 18-point
inspection if the roof already exists, or a **roofing takeoff from your plans** if it doesn't
yet. Either way you get photos or a sheet-by-sheet breakdown, real peso numbers, and a straight
answer on whether you need a repair, a care plan, a new install, or nothing at all. Normally
₱1,500 for an inspection, ₱3,500 for a takeoff.

**One promise:**
**We will tell you the truth about your roof — in writing, itemized, within 24 hours — even
when the truth costs us the job.**

## How you look and sound (design system)

**Brand colours:**

| Role | Hex | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Slate (primary) | `#16232E` | Headlines, footer, dark sections, body text |
| Storm (secondary) | `#2C4356` | Cards on light, hovers, subheads |
| Chalk (background) | `#F6F4EF` | Page background — warm off-white, never pure white |
| **Copper (accent/CTA)** | `#C4622D` | Buttons, links, rules, numbers, emphasis |
| Copper Light | `#E0975E` | Hover states, icon fills |
| Moss (support) | `#3E6B57` | Checkmarks, trust badges, "good news" states |
| Line | `#DCD7CC` | Borders, dividers |
| Ink Muted | `#5C6B75` | Captions, labels, secondary copy |

Warm chalk + slate + copper reads *craftsman*, not *contractor*. Every roofing and hardware
brand in the country is red, blue, or yellow — this palette alone is the fastest way to look
like nobody else in Laguna.

**Fonts:**
- **Headings:** `Fraunces` — serif, high contrast, slightly odd. Weights 600–700, tight
  tracking. Fallback: `Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif`.
- **Body / UI:** `Inter` — 400/500/600. Fallback: `-apple-system, 'Segoe UI', sans-serif`.
- **Labels & numbers:** Inter, uppercase, 12–13px, letter-spacing `0.08em`.
- Serif headline over sans body is the entire look. No third typeface.

**Voice — three words:**
**Straight. Specific. Unhurried.**

The voice of a competent kapitbahay who happens to know roofs and doesn't need the sale.
Short sentences. Real numbers in pesos. Name the actual part — *tekscrew, rubber washer, ridge
roll, flashing, C-purlin, canal, downspout, 0.5mm rib-type*. Describe consequences flatly and
let them land; never use fear as the lever. Admit things ("may mga bubong na talagang kailangan
nang palitan") because admission buys credibility. Dry humor once per asset, never at the
customer's expense.

**Language:** body copy in English — that's how Laguna subdivision homeowners read online. Ad
hooks, SMS, and Viber messages go **light Taglish**, because that's how they actually talk. Never
formal deep Filipino, never a full English script in an SMS. One or two Filipino phrases per
asset, used naturally, never as decoration.

*Sounds like us:*
> "A rubber washer costs eighteen pesos. The ceiling it ruins costs ₱25,000. Yan ang buong
> dahilan kung bakit may inspection."

*Never us:*
> "Don't let TYPHOON DAMAGE destroy your home! Call now for your FREE inspection!!"

**Never use:**
- ❌ The default GoHighLevel / ClickFunnels blue (`#2563EB`, `#0075FF`, and every cousin)
- ❌ Platform default fonts — Roboto, Open Sans, Montserrat, Poppins, system-UI-by-accident
- ❌ Generic stock photos: smiling families in front of houses, stock handshakes, headset
      call-center women, cartoon house icons
- ❌ Lorem ipsum, or any placeholder text shipped in a finished asset
- ❌ Roofer-red, safety-cone orange, starburst badges, "SALE" flags, countdown timers
- ❌ Exclamation points, ALL CAPS urgency, "Don't wait!", fake scarcity, 🔥💯🚨 in headlines
- ❌ Deep formal Filipino, or Taglish written by someone who doesn't speak it
- ❌ Drop shadows on buttons, gradient purple anything, drone-shot-with-lens-flare hero videos
- ❌ The capstone example businesses (gyms, med spas, solar, bookkeeping, real estate agencies)
- ❌ Any template default left untouched — if it shipped with the platform, it gets replaced

## The founder story (this is the trust asset — give it real space)

Rommel spent nine years running tear-off crews. The job that ended it: a two-storey house in
Cabuyao, ₱410,000 signed, and after the old panels came off the purlins underneath were clean.
No rust worth the name. That roof had eight, maybe ten years left. The actual problem had been a

That story goes on the landing page with a photo of hands on a panel. Not a footnote. It's the
reason anyone believes the 7-out-of-10 number, and it's the one thing no competitor can copy.

no fear-mongering required: *"We can fix this in April for ₱12,000, or we can tarp it in August
for more."*

**How we quote — say this out loud, it's a differentiator**
- Every quotation is **itemized**: gauge, sheet count, linear meters, fasteners, accessories,
  wastage allowance, labor, and price per square meter — each on its own line.
- **Valid 15 days.** Steel prices move; we won't pretend otherwise and we won't pad a number to
  cover the movement.
- Three ways to buy: **supply and install**, **labor only** with your own materials, or
  **supply only** at cost plus 8% handling. Most contractors here won't show you the difference.
- **VAT-inclusive, official receipt issued.** Not a cash-only crew.

For reference, the thing we argue against on existing roofs: a full long-span re-roof on a
typical Laguna two-storey (120–180 sqm of roof area) runs ₱180,000 – ₱450,000 all-in. When
someone gets quoted ₱380,000 for a roof that needed ₱16,000 of flashing work, that's the story
this whole business is built on.

## The system, end to end

```
Instagram carousel ┐
Facebook feed ad   ┘──► Landing page ──► Book-a-call calendar ──► GHL pipeline (6 stages)
                          (Trueline)      (15-min Roof Check)             │
                            │                                            ▼
                    "What do you need?"                    3 automations + 2 HTML emails
                    ├─ Leak or damage        → tag: repair       (follow-up branches by tag)
                    ├─ Maintenance plan      → tag: care-plan
                    ├─ Apartment / multi-unit→ tag: multi-unit
                    └─ New build, have plans → tag: new-build
```

One form, one calendar, one pipeline. A single **"What do you need?"** selector tags the lead on
submit, and the automations branch off that tag — same speed-to-lead engine, different second
touch. Homeowner leads get the repair-vs-re-roof nurture; multi-unit leads get the portfolio
angle; new-build leads get a plan-upload request so the takeoff can start before the call.

**Pipeline — "Trueline Roof Leads" (6 stages, built):**
1. New Lead → 2. Contacted → 3. Call Booked → 4. Assessment Done *(inspection or takeoff)* →
5. Quote Sent → 6. Won

Lost and nurture are handled by opportunity **status** + tag `nurture-may`, not by extra stages —
so a dead lead stays visible in the stage where it died and the board stays diagnostic.

Seeded demo leads should mix all three segments: roughly 9 homeowners, 3 multi-unit owners or
property managers, 3 new-build owners or small contractors — spread across every stage.

**Emails (pretty HTML, on-brand):**
1. **"Your roof check is booked"** — confirmation: what happens on the call, what to have ready
   (a photo of the ceiling stain, the other contractor's quote, or the roof plan if it's a new
   build), and the 24-hour itemized quotation promise.
2. **"6 signs it's a repair, not a re-roof"** — nurture for leads who landed and didn't book.
   Same spine as the carousel, so the whole system speaks with one voice.

**Automations (3, designed here):**
1. **Speed-to-Lead** — form submit → SMS + email inside 60 seconds → 5-minute call task for
   Rommel → branch on tag (new-build leads get a plan-upload request; multi-unit get the
   portfolio note) → no booking in 24h, send email #2 → no booking in 4 days, second SMS in
   Taglish → move to Nurture.
2. **Appointment Confirm & Show-Up** — call booked → move to Call Booked → send email #1 →
   SMS reminder 24h before → SMS 1h before → no-show fires a two-touch rescue back to Contacted.
3. **Quote Follow-Up / Stall-Breaker** — Quote Sent → day 3 "any questions on the quotation?" →
   day 7 flat seasonal note (fix it in April vs. tarp it in August; close in the roof before the
   rains) → day 14 to Nurture with a pre-rainy-season check-in tag for May.

**Targets to quote in the pitch (illustrative):** 18–25% click-to-booked-call · under 60s
speed-to-lead · ~80% show rate with two reminders · ~70% call-to-assessment · ~55%
assessment-to-paid · ₱200–450 cost per booked call on Meta against a ₱16,800 average repair,
much higher on multi-unit and new-build.
