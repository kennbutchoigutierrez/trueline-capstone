## Visual continuity system

The thing that makes these read as one set is that **every sign slide is the same
document**: same rail, same plate, same two fact rows, same signature.

- **Palette** — Chalk `#F6F4EF` ground, Slate `#16232E` type and linework, Copper
  `#C4622D` reserved for one thing only: *the failure*. Nothing else on any slide is
  copper except the price, which is the point. Moss `#3E6B57` appears exactly once, on
  the sound fastener in Sign 02, so "good" has a colour too.
- **Typography** — Fraunces 700 for every headline and every peso figure; Inter for
  labels (19px, uppercase, `0.14em`) and body (31px). No third face, per the brief.
- **Recurring object** — the bordered **diagram plate**. It carries one cross-section per
  slide and appears on all six sign slides in the same position at the same size.
- **Layout rhythm** — rail → headline → plate → *what it usually means* → *usually costs*
  → signature rule. The plate flexes so the two fact rows land at the same height on
  every slide, which is what makes the swipe feel like turning pages rather than
  scrolling posts.
- **Dark inversions** — slides 1, 8 and 9 run on Slate. The inversion marks the three
  structural beats: the story, the counterweight, the ask.
- **No characters, no stock photography.** One photograph total, on slide 1.

**Why diagrams instead of photographs.** The six signs are specific physical faults and
I had no photographs of them. Sourcing generic roof stock would have meant illustrating
"rust around a tekscrew" with a picture of something else, which is the exact dishonesty
this brand is built against. Drawn cross-sections also let the slides *name the part* —
tekscrew, EPDM washer, purlin, ridge roll, side lap, canal — which is the voice in
`brief.md` made visual. No roofing competitor in this market publishes technical drawings.

## Slide by slide

### Slide 1 — Hook
- **Purpose:** open the loop with the founder story, which is the one asset no competitor can copy.
- **Kicker:** Cabuyao, 2018 · the job that started this
- **Headline:** ₱410,000 for a roof that had ten years left.
- **Supporting:** We took the old panels off and the purlins underneath were clean. The actual problem was two meters of unsealed ridge roll.
- **Swipe cue:** Six signs it's a repair, not a re-roof →
- **Visual:** photo band over solid Slate. Dark long-span roof, shot from below. The headline never sits on texture.

### Slide 2 — Sign 01
- **Headline:** The stain that only shows in heavy rain
- **Means:** Water is entering somewhere else and running along a purlin. The leak is rarely above the stain.
- **Costs:** ₱6,500 – ₱14,000
- **Visual:** entry point on the panel, dashed path down and along the purlin, stain blooming on the ceiling board a long way from where the water got in.

### Slide 3 — Sign 02
- **Headline:** Rust blooming around the tekscrews
- **Means:** The fastener seal went first, not the panel. Rust starts at the hole and spreads outward from there.
- **Costs:** ₱7,500 – ₱18,000
- **Visual:** panel from above, three fastener heads, copper bloom on two, a moss ring on the sound one.

### Slide 4 — Sign 03
- **Headline:** Dead rubber washers
- **Means:** The rubber perishes in about eight years. The panel it sits on is usually still sound.
- **Costs:** ₱6,500 – ₱12,000
- **Visual:** the hero diagram. Tekscrew enlarged ×12, EPDM washer split open, callout reading **₱18 each**. This is the brief's "₱18 part that ruins ₱25,000 ceilings" made literal.

### Slide 5 — Sign 04
- **Headline:** A panel lifting at the side lap
- **Means:** Wind got under the overlap and worked it loose. Refasten and reseal the lap and the panel stays.
- **Costs:** ₱9,000 – ₱22,000
- **Visual:** cross-section, copper wedge where the upper panel has lifted off the seated one, habagat arrow coming in from the right.

### Slide 6 — Sign 05
- **Headline:** A ridge roll nobody has sealed
- **Means:** The cap is on, the sealant bead underneath is not. Heavy rain gets driven straight in at the apex.
- **Costs:** ₱6,500 – ₱15,000
- **Visual:** apex cross-section. The void where the bead should be is filled copper on both sides; rain tracks under the cap and falls through the open ridge.

### Slide 7 — Sign 06
- **Headline:** A canal rusted through at the bottom
- **Means:** The gutter fails long before the roof does. It sits in standing water; the panels do not.
- **Costs:** ₱8,500 – ₱28,000
- **Visual:** gutter cross-section holding standing water, broken open at its lowest point, draining where it should not.

### Slide 8 — The counterweight
- **Headline:** 2 signs you really do need a re-roof
- **Item 1 — The purlins are rusted through.** When the steel holding the roof up flakes in your hand, new panels have nothing to bite into.
- **Item 2 — Pinholes across the whole run.** Not two spots. The whole sheet has gone thin, and patching it buys you one dry season.
- **Closing:** When that is the answer, we say it, hand you to one of two contractors we trust, and make nothing.
- **Visual:** inverted to Slate. Two small copper-marked icons. This slide is why the other six are believable.

### Slide 9 — CTA
- **Kicker:** Normally ₱1,500 · free this month across Laguna
- **Headline:** Find out which one you have.
- **Body:** A free 18-point roof check, and an itemized written quotation in your inbox within 24 hours. Photos, real peso numbers, and a straight answer — even when the answer costs us the job.
- **CTA:** Book a 15-minute roof check → · Link in bio

The headline resolves the whole carousel: six signs pointing one way, two pointing the

## ⚠ Numbers to confirm before this goes live

`brief.md` publishes the **band** (repairs ₱6,500 – ₱75,000, average ₱16,800) but not
per-symptom prices. **The six ranges on slides 2–7 are new** — I set them inside the
published band and kept the average plausible, but Rommel should sign off before posting,
since the whole positioning rests on the numbers being real.

One knock-on: the founder story says the Cabuyao ridge roll was "about ₱6,000 of work",
while Sign 05 starts at ₱6,500 to respect the published floor. Both appear in this
carousel (slide 1 and slide 6). It reads as one job coming in slightly under the usual
range, which is fine, but worth knowing it's there.

## Rebuilding

```
python carousel/build-fonts.py     # once — inlines Fraunces + Inter as base64
python carousel/render.py          # all nine slides
python carousel/render.py 4 8      # just slides 4 and 8
```

Fonts are embedded into `slides/fonts.css` as base64, including the `latin-ext` subset —
that subset is what carries the peso sign, so dropping it makes every price fall back to
a system font mid-string. Rendering is headless Chrome at a 1:1 device pixel ratio; the
PNGs come out at exactly 1080 × 1350 with no network access required.
